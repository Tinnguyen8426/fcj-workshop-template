Reflecting on my 12-week internship journey within the **First Cloud AI Journey (FCAJ)** program organized by **AWS Vietnam** (from **May 04, 2026** to **July 25, 2026**), I gained an invaluable opportunity to bridge the gap between academic theory and real-world execution.

Throughout this period, my primary technical focus was exploring the AWS Cloud ecosystem—spanning IAM security profiles, Serverless FaaS paradigms (AWS Lambda), application containerization via Docker/Amazon ECR, and integrating the `aws-serverless-java-container-springboot3` framework. As a key outcome, I successfully engineered the core **GearStore** service on **Spring Boot 3**, resolved dependency overhead using `maven-shade-plugin` for optimized Shaded JAR compilation, built the `StreamLambdaHandler.java` router to handle Binary Streams, and seamlessly deployed the serverless application to AWS Lambda.

In terms of work ethic, I strictly adhered to the weekly roadmap laid out in my worklog, took initiative in analyzing technical documentation and cloud logs, and actively collaborated with team members to push project goals forward.

Despite achieving significant milestones, I recognize key areas where I can continue to grow:

*   **Strengthening Self-Discipline & Workflow Adherence:** I aim to align even more strictly with Git branching strategies, Maven dependency guidelines, and sprint deadlines. Additionally, I will optimize my schedule to balance theoretical lab research with hands-on Spring Boot Serverless development to maintain steady progress.
*   **Refining Troubleshooting & Log Analysis Skills:** When facing complex technical hurdles—such as JVM Cold Start latency, binary stream mapping mismatches, or Maven artifact packaging conflicts—I plan to streamline my diagnostic workflow. By leveraging Amazon CloudWatch logs more methodically, I will reduce root-cause isolation time.
*   **Enhancing Technical Communication & Flexibility:** I intend to practice explaining intricate concepts (like FaaS mechanics or Serverless Java Containers) more concisely to team members. Furthermore, I will remain receptive to constructive peer review during architecture discussions to foster effective decision-making.
